## Discussion

### "Small" changes — attack the problem on several fronts
- **Initialisation** (already done): clusters vs. spread-out starts;
  Latin-hypercube is "very reasonable" per Arthur.
- **Constraint handling**: move from random repositioning to
  reflection/clipping. Arthur: "makes sense, good idea."
- **Recombination**:
  - A novel way of *selecting one recombination operator over another*.
  - Does the **symmetry** of CiaS affect the recombination?
  - Maybe use several recombination operators in the same scheme.
  - On strategy parameters: optimise individually, or pick the
    parameter that performs best and recombine only that one.
- **Strategy-parameter selection**: at what point does one σ-strategy
  (single / multiple / full covariance) outperform another? Do we need
  all three throughout development, or commit to one? Does introducing a
  specific strategy parameter make sense only in specific scenarios?

### Elitism (nuanced)
- Tournament/truncation selection already lets the best solution compete
  in at least one tournament, so **the best is rarely lost** — a plain
  elitist archive is then mostly *bookkeeping*. If the algorithm
  converges anyway without it, is it really an improvement?
- More interesting: **reinsert archived solutions back into the
  population** when the algorithm stalls — the archive becomes a
  *backup population* and helps preserve diversity (Arthur liked this
  framing; addresses the big-diversity-loss concern).
- Deliverable: one plot for the plain archive, one for archive +
  reintroduction, to show whether the algorithm actually *uses* it.

### Selection schemes
- Compare `(μ, λ)` vs `(μ + λ)`.
- Arthur: if we compare specific selection operators we need a *reason*
  for choosing them — otherwise compare all of them.

### "Big" change
- **Gradient + EA hybrid loop**: the EA does the broad/global search,
  a gradient-based local optimiser does the fine/local search.

## Decisions / rules of engagement
- **Statistical significance**: report p-value tests almost everywhere
  (baseline vs. improvement) to show results are valid.
- **Justify direction**: every improvement needs a reason; no blindly
  trying things.
- **Bugs ≠ improvements**: bug fixes (e.g. τ-operator, `(μ, μ)` ratio)
  are tracked and delivered to the TA so he can hand a working codebase
  to next year's group — but they are *not* presented as results.
- **Speed up**: first round of improvements documented and delivered by
  end of week.
- Next TA meeting is **pre-presentation** — almost everything complete.
